OPEN LEARNING GUIDE

What is scattering?

Scattering proposes many XZ positions, asks the terrain whether a surface exists there, and places only accepted instances.

Candidate vs. accepted

A candidate is an attempt. An accepted point has a downward ray intersection; a rejected point lies outside the mesh or has no valid hit.

Why use a seed?

The seed initializes a deterministic random sequence. The same seed and parameters repeat the same candidates, scales, and yaw values.

Why query the surface?

A fixed Y ignores hills and valleys. This round casts down onto the rendered height-field mesh and uses the first intersection.

Why InstancedMesh?

All trunks share one geometry and material; all crowns share another. Per-instance matrices vary position, scale, and yaw without one Mesh per tree.

What is still weak?

Uniform random points can cluster and overlap. There are no slope, biome, shoreline, spacing, or multi-species rules yet.
